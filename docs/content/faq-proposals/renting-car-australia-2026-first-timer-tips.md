## Q1: How far in advance should you book a rental car in Australia?
Lock in bookings at least 8–10 weeks ahead for peak dates such as mid-December through late January, Easter, June–July school holidays, and September–October. Most major providers allow free cancellation up to 48 hours out, so booking early and re-shopping closer to the date carries no downside.

## Q2: Do you need extra insurance when renting a car in Australia?
Headline daily rates rarely include meaningful cover, leaving a standard excess of roughly AUD $3,000–$5,500. Counter excess reduction costs about $25–$40 per day, while third-party providers like RentalCover.com and iCarHireInsurance charge $8–$12 daily for zero-excess cover, typically saving $20–$30 per day with broader protection.

## Q3: How do toll roads work for rental cars in Australia?
Tolling is fully electronic with no booths; cameras read the plate and the rental company bills the toll plus an admin fee, commonly $3.30–$5.50 per day used. Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane have tolled networks, while Adelaide, Perth, Hobart, Darwin and Canberra have none. Expect roughly $25–$50 for a one-week Sydney rental.

## Q4: Do you need a 4WD to drive in Australia?
Not for major routes — the Great Ocean Road, Pacific Coast, the sealed Red Centre Way to Uluru, and Tasmania's main loop all suit a 2WD sedan or small SUV. Genuine 4WD territory includes Fraser Island, the Gibb River Road, Cape York north of Cooktown, and most standard contracts forbid unsealed roads entirely.

## Q5: What extra fees apply to young drivers renting a car in Australia?
Renters under 25 face a young driver surcharge of roughly $25–$35 per day across the whole rental, with minimum age generally 21, though Europcar and some Hertz locations rent to 18-year-olds at a steeper rate. Additional drivers cost about $7–$15 daily, and the licence presented must be physical, not digital.
