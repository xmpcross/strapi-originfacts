## Q1: How much do castle hotels in Germany cost?
Many serious German Burghotels sit in the €150–€280 per night range, even on the Rhine and Mosel, thanks to enormous supply — over 20,000 castles, palaces, and fortifications — and family operators setting prices. Off-season stays from November to mid-March, excluding Christmas markets, often drop rates a further 25–40%.

## Q2: Are German castle hotels cheaper than French châteaux?
Yes — a two-night Loire stay at somewhere like Château de Bagnols can run €800–€1,200 per night in high season, while comparable German Burghotels commonly charge €150–€280. Germany's larger supply of castles and family operators keep prices lower, though service tends to be warm rather than slick.

## Q3: Which German castle hotels are easy to reach from Frankfurt?
Burghotel auf Schönburg sits about 75 minutes by regional train from Frankfurt Hbf to Oberwesel, followed by a steep ten-minute walk or a shuttle if called ahead. Schlosshotel Kronberg is twenty minutes from Frankfurt's airport on the S4 S-Bahn line, making it a convenient jet-lag landing pad at €280–€500 per night.

## Q4: How can you save money booking German castle hotels?
Book directly — most Burghotels honor Booking.com rates but add breakfast or a free upgrade for phone or email bookings roughly one time in three. Fly into secondary airports like Frankfurt-Hahn, Memmingen, or Düsseldorf to save $150–$300 round-trip, and avoid German school holidays, which spike rates 20–30%.

## Q5: What are the drawbacks of staying in a German castle hotel?
Many castles have uneven Wi-Fi, no air conditioning, and stairs instead of elevators, and family-run service is warm but not slick — no Four Seasons-style turndown in a 12th-century keep. Sunday check-ins in small towns are also risky, since restaurants and shops shut hard; arrive Monday or eat on-site.
