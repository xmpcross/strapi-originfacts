## Q1: How much does an Angkor Wat pass cost?
As of 2024 pricing, a 1-day pass costs $37, a 3-day pass $62 (usable over any 10 days), and a 7-day pass $72 (usable over any month). The 3-day pass suits almost everyone beyond a single sunrise visit. Buy only at the official ticket window — there are no legitimate third-party sellers.

## Q2: What is the best time to visit Angkor Wat to avoid crowds?
April or September. High season runs November through February, when European and Chinese tour groups converge on Siem Reap. April is hot, above 38°C, and September falls in the rainy season's tail, but showers are short and the wet-season light is extraordinary. Boutique guesthouses drop under $60 per night versus $120-plus in December.

## Q3: How do you beat the crowds at Angkor Wat sunrise?
Arrive around 5:30am but skip the packed reflecting pool on the causeway's north side; the library pavilion at the end of the right-side causeway is often nearly empty. The real advantage is being inside the inner galleries by 6:15am and leaving by 8:30, before tour groups arrive between 7:30 and 9:00am.

## Q4: Is hiring a guide at Angkor Wat worth it?
Yes, for at least one day. A licensed guide with a tuk-tuk typically costs $30-50 for a full day through the Khmer Angkor Tour Guide Association or platforms like Klook. Guides know which gates tour buses use, explain the nearly 800 meters of bas-reliefs, and track which temples are under restoration. Choose someone with five-plus years of experience.

## Q5: How do you get to Siem Reap and what does the visa cost?
Bangkok and Kuala Lumpur are the main connecting hubs, with AirAsia and Bangkok Airways flying frequent Siem Reap routes — budget $80-150 round-trip from Bangkok booked 4-6 weeks out. A tuk-tuk from Siem Reap International Airport to the Old Market runs $7-10. The Cambodia e-Visa costs $36 and processes in three business days.
