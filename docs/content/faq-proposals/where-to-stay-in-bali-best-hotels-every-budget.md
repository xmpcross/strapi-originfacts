## Q1: Which area of Bali should you stay in?
It depends on the trip: Seminyak suits polished beach clubs and restaurants, Canggu is the digital-nomad hub with budget-to-boutique stays, Ubud covers rice terraces and wellness retreats without a beach, Uluwatu delivers clifftop luxury and surf, while Amed, Lovina, and Sidemen offer quieter, cheaper coasts. Kuta works only for a jet-lag night.

## Q2: How much do budget hotels in Bali cost?
Canggu guesthouses along Jalan Batu Bolong regularly come in under $30 per night for a clean private room with air conditioning, hot shower, and breakfast, often with a small pool. Selina Bali dorm beds run $15–$20, with privates at $45–$65. Equivalent Seminyak budget stays cost roughly 40–60% more.

## Q3: When is the best time to book hotels in Bali?
Dry season (April–October) is peak, pushing luxury rates 30–50% above low season; July–August requires booking three to four months out for Ubud or Seminyak. Wet season (November–March) brings afternoon downpours and big discounts — February is the quietest month, with some villas dropping up to 40% off peak rates.

## Q4: Are private villas cheaper than hotels in Bali?
For groups of three or more, villas almost always win on cost per person. A three-bedroom Seminyak pool villa with cook and driver runs $250–$400 per night total — split three ways, $85–$130 each, rivaling mid-range hotel rates while including a private pool, daily breakfast, and airport-run driver.

## Q5: Is it better to book Bali hotels directly or through OTAs?
Booking directly with villas often beats OTA rates, especially for stays of five nights or more, and villa managers typically answer WhatsApp inquiries within hours. Agoda and Booking.com carry the widest guesthouse inventory, with Agoda often slightly cheaper for Indonesian properties. Watch for non-refundable deposits of 30–50% on many villas.
