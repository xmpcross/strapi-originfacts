## Q1: What is the best time to visit Bangkok for first-timers?
Mid-November through late January is the sweet spot, when highs sit in the low-to-mid 30s Celsius and humidity eases. March through May is brutal at 38-40°C, while June-October brings 30-90 minute afternoon downpours. Because this window coincides with peak season, book flights early — U.S. West Coast round-trips can exceed $1,000 by August.

## Q2: How do you get from Suvarnabhumi Airport to central Bangkok?
The Airport Rail Link reaches Phaya Thai station in about 30 minutes for around 45 baht — roughly $1.25. A metered taxi runs $10-15 plus a 50-baht expressway surcharge; skip the fixed-price "official" taxi touts inside the terminal and use the metered taxi queue on the ground floor instead.

## Q3: How much does accommodation cost in Bangkok?
Budget guesthouses in the Banglamphu/Khao San area start under $20 per night for a private room, though they sit far from BTS access. Solid 4-star hotels near Silom or Sukhumvit run $50-100 per night, while five-star properties like Capella Bangkok or Rosewood Bangkok often come in under $250 — far less than London or Tokyo equivalents.

## Q4: What common scams should first-timers in Bangkok avoid?
The classic scam involves a well-dressed local near the Grand Palace claiming it is closed for a ceremony, then steering visitors by tuk-tuk to an overpriced gem shop or tailor. Decline anyone who initiates contact near a major sight and offers to redirect you — the Grand Palace closes only on Thai national holidays.

## Q5: What is the dress code for Bangkok's royal temples?
Dress code is strictly enforced: shoulders must be covered with no tank tops, knees must be covered with no short shorts, and flip-flops are barred inside certain temple buildings. Shawls are sometimes available to borrow at the entrance, but visitors should not count on it. The Grand Palace admission costs 500 baht, about $14.
