# FAQ proposal — What to Pack for Thailand: The Only Checklist You'll Need

## Q1: What should you wear for temple visits in Thailand?
Temples require covered shoulders and knees — Wat Phra Kaew and similar sites enforce this — so pack one or two pairs of lightweight trousers or linen pants, which also help on overnight trains. A thin long-sleeve layer is useful too, since air conditioning in buses, flights, and malls can be aggressive.

## Q2: Can you buy toiletries and clothing cheaply in Thailand?
Yes — Thailand is among the cheapest places to buy clothing, toiletries, and gear. Chatuchak Weekend Market in Bangkok sells linen shirts and flip-flops for a few dollars, and widespread pharmacies like Boots and Watsons stock sunscreen, repellent, and most over-the-counter medications. One caveat: market clothing sizing often runs small by Western standards.

## Q3: What health items should you bring from home to Thailand?
Bring prescription medications with a copy of the prescription, reef-safe SPF 50+ sunscreen (local versions often contain whitening agents), DEET or picaridin repellent since dengue is a real rainy-season risk, oral rehydration salts, a small first-aid kit, and water purification backup — tap water in Thailand is not safe to drink.

## Q4: What luggage works best for a Thailand trip?
A 40–50-litre carry-on-sized backpack or roller — such as the Osprey Farpoint 40 — is the sweet spot for two-to-three-week trips; anything larger becomes inconvenient. Add a 15–20-litre daypack for day trips and a dry bag around $20 for boat days. Backpacks suit multi-destination trips; rollers suit longer Bangkok stays.

## Q5: Do US plugs work in Thailand?
Thailand uses Type A and B outlets — the same flat two-pin plugs as the US — running at 220V, so American devices plug in without an adapter but must be dual-voltage. Modern laptops, phone chargers, and camera batteries usually are; older hair dryers and straighteners often are not, so check labels.
