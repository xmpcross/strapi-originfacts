# FAQ proposal — How to Fly to Melbourne for Under $900 Return in 2026

## Q1: Which airlines fly to Melbourne for under $900 return?
Realistic sub-$900 options include Jetstar's LAX–MEL nonstop at $780–$880 for shoulder dates, Fiji Airways from SFO via Nadi at $820–$890, and Air New Zealand from Seattle via Auckland at $820–$900. Scoot via Singapore, plus wildcards China Airlines and Philippine Airlines, occasionally drop into the $780–$950 range.

## Q2: When is the cheapest time to fly to Melbourne?
The cheapest windows are early February through late March, late April through mid-June when fares hit their annual floor, and late October through early December. Peak demand runs mid-December through late January, plus Easter and the AFL Grand Final, when fares spike 40–60 percent. Departures between December 15 and January 5 cost $1,800-plus.

## Q3: How far in advance should you book flights to Melbourne?
Australia–US fares typically bottom out three to five months before departure, with a smaller second dip about six to eight weeks out — the common advice to book eleven months ahead is wrong for this route. Set price alerts, track fares for at least three weeks, and book the day a fare drops 15 percent or more below baseline.

## Q4: Which US airport is best for cheap flights to Melbourne?
LAX offers by far the most options, including Jetstar's nonstop plus Fiji, Air New Zealand, Qantas, United, Philippine, and China Airlines. SFO is a strong second. Travelers east of the Mississippi should consider a cheap positioning flight to LAX, since East Coast connecting itineraries add six to ten hours and often cost more.

## Q5: What extra fees can push up a cheap Melbourne fare?
Add-ons can turn a $799 headline fare into $1,050. Jetstar charges roughly $50–$90 per checked bag each direction, seat selection runs $15–$45 per segment, and pre-ordered meals cost about $20. Travel insurance runs $40–$80 for two weeks, and US passport holders need an Electronic Travel Authority costing AUD $20.
