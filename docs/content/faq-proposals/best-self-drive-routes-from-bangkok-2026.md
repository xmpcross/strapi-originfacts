## Q1: What do you need to rent a car in Bangkok?
An International Driving Permit is required alongside a home licence, obtained from a national automobile association before departure. A compact automatic from Avis, Hertz, Budget, or Sixt at Suvarnabhumi Airport typically runs $35–$55 per day, with full CDW coverage adding $10–$18 daily. Book at least two weeks ahead in high season.

## Q2: What are the best self-drive routes from Bangkok?
Four routes stand out: Kanchanaburi and the River Kwai, about 130 km west and the most forgiving for first-timers; Khao Yai National Park, roughly 190 km northeast and best over two nights; Hua Hin on the Gulf coast, around 200 km south; and Koh Samet, under 220 km east via the Ban Phe ferry pier.

## Q3: When is the best time for a road trip from Bangkok?
November through February is the recommended window: dry weather, manageable temperatures, and national park wildlife most active in cooler mornings. March and April get extremely hot around Kanchanaburi, while May through October brings rain. Avoid Songkran in mid-April and the Loy Krathong long weekend, when every highway out of Bangkok saturates.

## Q4: Is it worth driving a rental car inside Bangkok itself?
No. Traffic on Sukhumvit Road during the morning rush can leave drivers stationary for 45 minutes over three kilometres, so the BTS Skytrain and BRT are better within the city. The recommended approach is picking the car up only when leaving town and returning it at the airport on the way out.

## Q5: What precautions should drivers take on Thai roads?
Photograph the car thoroughly at pickup, since rental dispute rates are high. Download offline maps before leaving the city, carry Thai baht cash for rural petrol stations and park gates, and avoid night driving because of free-roaming animals and unlit motorbikes. Confirm the fuel type, as most rental compacts take 91 octane or E20.
