## Q1: How much does budget travel in Bangkok cost per day?
Bangkok stretches money far: hostel dorms in Banglamphu or Silom run $8–$15 with air conditioning and lockers, guesthouse private rooms $18–$25, and mid-range hotels near BTS lines $50–$100. Total food spend of $15–$20 daily is achievable, with street meals under 60 THB and BTS rides costing roughly 50 cents.

## Q2: When are flights to Bangkok cheapest?
Shoulder season runs roughly mid-January through February and late August through early October, when round-trip economy fares from Los Angeles regularly dip below $700 on carriers like Cathay Pacific, Korean Air, and Japan Airlines. East Coast departures add $150–$200. A Google Flights alert with a flexible ±3-day window catches the first drops.

## Q3: What is the cheapest way to get around Bangkok?
The BTS Skytrain and MRT are almost always faster than taxis at peak hours and dramatically cheaper, with a cross-city ride costing roughly 50 cents. A Rabbit Card speeds boarding, and the 30-Day Unlimited Pass lowers per-trip costs further. The Chao Phraya Express orange-flag boat charges a flat 15 THB and stops near major temples.

## Q4: When is the cheapest time to visit Bangkok?
June through October, the rainy season, brings the lowest accommodation rates, with afternoon downpours but often-clear mornings; a mid-September arrival can pair shoulder-season airfares with cheap rooms and manageable weather. November through February is dry, cooler, and more expensive. During Songkran, April 13–15, accommodation prices triple and transport gets chaotic.

## Q5: Where should budget travelers stay in Bangkok?
Silom or lower Sukhumvit beats Khao San Road for most visitors: Khao San has genuine backpacker energy but sits a 20-minute taxi ride from most cultural and culinary highlights, while Silom and Sukhumvit put travelers on BTS lines for only marginally more money. Transit savings often offset the slight room-rate difference.
