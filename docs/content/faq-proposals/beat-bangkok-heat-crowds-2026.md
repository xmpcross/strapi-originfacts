## Q1: When is the best time to visit Bangkok to avoid heat and crowds?
November through February is the sweet spot, with temperatures at a manageable 28-32°C, lower humidity, and rare rain — though hotel rates run higher and major temples are busiest. March-May brings extreme heat but thinner crowds and cheaper rooms. The window genuinely worth avoiding is Songkran in mid-April, when hotels can triple in price.

## Q2: How should you structure a day in Bangkok's heat?
Flip the usual schedule: do outdoor sightseeing from 6-9am when temple grounds are cool and near-empty, move to air-conditioned museums like BACC or the Jim Thompson House until 1pm, wait out the 1-4pm "kill zone" in malls such as Siam Paragon or Terminal 21, then return outside from 4:30pm onward.

## Q3: How do you avoid crowds at the Grand Palace?
Arrive at the 8:30am opening — the first 90 minutes are meaningfully calmer before tour buses arrive. The Grand Palace does not currently offer timed-entry reservations, so check the official website before traveling. Cover shoulders and knees, since the dress code is strictly enforced, and visit Wat Pho separately rather than combining both in one hot morning.

## Q4: What is the best way to get around Bangkok?
The BTS Skytrain covers major hotel and shopping districts with fares of 17-59 baht, interchanging with the MRT Blue Line at Asok/Sukhumvit. Grab is cheaper than street-hailed taxis with fixed fares. Avoid tuk-tuks beyond a short novelty ride, and consider the underrated Saen Saeb canal boat, which crosses congested central Bangkok for under 20 baht.

## Q5: Where should first-timers stay in Bangkok?
Sukhumvit between BTS Nana and Phrom Phong offers walkable BTS access and dozens of restaurants, with mid-range hotels at $80-150 per night in peak season and less in hot months. Riverside near ICONSIAM suits repeat visitors seeking local character, while staying in the Old City means depending on ferries, tuk-tuks, and Grab for transport.
