## Q1: Which Southeast Asian border crossings are best for independent travelers in 2026?
Five well-documented routes stand out: Thailand to Laos via the First Friendship Bridge at Nong Khai, Cambodia to Vietnam at Moc Bai–Bavet, Vietnam to Cambodia by river at Vinh Xuong–Kaam Samnor, Malaysia to Singapore across the Woodlands Causeway, and Singapore to Indonesia by ferry from Tanah Merah to Batam.

## Q2: How much do visas cost for a Southeast Asia overland circuit?
Laos visa-on-arrival ran roughly $35–$42 in 2025 depending on nationality, payable in USD, Thai Baht, or Lao Kip. Vietnam's e-visa costs $25 for 90-day entry, Cambodia's e-visa is $36 including processing, and Indonesia's visa-on-arrival at Batam is $35, though over 150 nationalities enter Indonesia visa-free.

## Q3: What documents should be prepared before crossing Southeast Asian borders?
A passport with at least six months' validity beyond entry, printed copies of Vietnam and Cambodia e-visas, at least four current 35mm x 45mm passport photos, $100 or more in small USD bills, a completed SG Arrival Card before reaching Singapore, and travel insurance documentation, since some countries are moving toward requiring proof.

## Q4: What is the best way to cross from Johor Bahru into Singapore?
The KTM Komuter train from JB Sentral to Woodlands CIQ is fast, air-conditioned, costs under $5 SGD, and sidesteps the bus queue chaos. The Causeway Link CW1 bus costs roughly $3–$4 SGD but can involve 90-minute queues on Friday evenings. Midweek mornings are the sweet spot for crossing.

## Q5: How long does the full overland circuit take?
The circuit from Bangkok through Vientiane, Phnom Penh, Ho Chi Minh City, Singapore, and Batam takes a minimum of three weeks done properly. Starting with the Vietnam e-visa is advised because it takes the longest — up to three business days, occasionally more — and anchors the Ho Chi Minh City leg.
