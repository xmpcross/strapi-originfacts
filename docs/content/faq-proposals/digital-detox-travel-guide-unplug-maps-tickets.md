# FAQ proposal — Digital Detox Travel: Unplug Without Losing Maps or Tickets

## Q1: How can you navigate without mobile data on a trip?
Download Google Maps offline areas for the destination city plus day-trip zones before leaving home; offline maps expire after about 15 days and refresh over Wi-Fi. Organic Maps or Maps.me serve as free, fully offline backups that handle hiking trails and rural roads better than Google's offline layer.

## Q2: Do mobile tickets and transit cards work in Airplane Mode?
Transit cards stored in Apple Wallet or Google Wallet — such as Suica and PASMO for Tokyo, OMNY for New York, and Oyster for London — work even in Airplane Mode because NFC still fires. Boarding passes should also be saved to Wallet plus a PDF screenshot rather than relying on airline apps alone.

## Q3: What phone settings help reduce screen time while traveling?
Set social apps to a one-minute daily limit through iOS Screen Time or Android Digital Wellbeing — the three-tap friction kills about 90% of idle opens. Enabling grayscale via a side-button triple-click makes feeds genuinely boring, and notifications should be limited to messages from favorites, the airline, hotel, and ride-hailing apps.

## Q4: What should a paper backup for travel include?
A single printed A4 sheet folded into the passport should list flight numbers, times, and confirmation codes for both directions; hotel names, addresses in the local language, and phone numbers; the embassy address in the destination city; one emergency contact; and the travel insurance policy number with its 24-hour assistance line.

## Q5: How much does a travel eSIM cost?
A 5 GB regional Europe eSIM plan from providers like Airalo, Holafly, or Saily runs roughly $15–25 for 30 days. Activation should happen on home Wi-Fi before departure, since some eSIMs need a working connection to activate, but switching it on as the data line can wait until landing.
