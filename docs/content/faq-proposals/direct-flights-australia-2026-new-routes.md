# FAQ proposal — Direct Flights to Australia in 2026: The New Routes Worth Booking

## Q1: What new direct flights to Australia are launching in 2026?
Qantas Project Sunrise nonstops from Sydney to London Heathrow and New York JFK on the A350-1000ULR target a 2026 launch at around 19–20 hours flying time. A Perth–Paris seasonal nonstop is confirmed for the northern summer of 2026, while United and American keep expanding US–Australia service.

## Q2: How much do flights to Australia cost from the US?
Realistic economy round-trips run $1,100–$1,500 from the West Coast in shoulder season and $1,800–$2,400 in peak; East Coast fares run $1,400–$1,800 and $2,200–$3,000 respectively. Premium economy typically costs 2.2–2.6 times economy, and early Project Sunrise business class estimates sit around $9,000–$12,000 round-trip.

## Q3: When is the cheapest time to fly to Australia?
Late April through early June and the first three weeks of September are the cheapest months from the US. December 10 through January 15 should be avoided, since Christmas and Australian school holidays double fares. The booking sweet spot is three to five months out; last-minute deals are rare.

## Q4: How long is the flight from the US to Australia?
SFO–Sydney runs about 15 hours westbound and around 13.5 hours eastbound, LAX–Melbourne about 15.5 hours, and Dallas/Fort Worth to Sydney or Brisbane roughly 17 hours westbound — the longest scheduled route from the contiguous US. Honolulu–Sydney on Hawaiian is shortest at around 10.5 hours.

## Q5: Is flying to Australia via Asia cheaper than nonstop?
Economy routing through Asia can cost 30–50% less, with VietJet round-trips under $700 common in shoulder seasons and Singapore–Perth on Scoot dropping to $180–$250 one-way when booked 8–10 weeks out. Total travel time often isn't much shorter than a US nonstop once connections are counted.
