# FAQ proposal — Cheap Hotels in Tokyo: Best Areas for Budget Travelers

## Q1: How much do budget hotels in Tokyo cost per night?
Clean, well-located rooms are available for under $80 a night with planning. Capsule hotels run $30–$55 for solo travelers, budget business hotels like Toyoko Inn, APA, and Dormy Inn charge $60–$100 for a double, and hostels with private rooms fall between $45 and $75 per night.

## Q2: What is the best area to stay in Tokyo on a budget?
Asakusa offers the best balance of character and affordability, with business hotel doubles at $60–$90 and capsule hotels from $30–$45, plus direct Ginza Line access. Shinjuku, Ueno, and the overlooked Akihabara–Asakusabashi area are also strong, with Akihabara typically $10–$20 cheaper per night than comparable Shinjuku properties.

## Q3: When are Tokyo hotels cheapest?
Mid-January through late February is Tokyo's genuine off-season, along with late June through mid-July during the rainy season and September after the summer heat breaks. Avoid late March through mid-April cherry blossom season, Golden Week (roughly April 29–May 5), and mid-October through early November; timing can shift nightly costs 30–50%.

## Q4: Are capsule hotels in Tokyo worth it?
Capsule hotels cost $30–$55 per night solo and have evolved far beyond the boxy 1980s pods, with private sleeping pods and often high-quality shared bathrooms. Most have gender-separated floors, making them excellent for solo travelers, but pods lack lockable doors and the format is awkward for couples.

## Q5: Should you book Tokyo hotels direct or through booking sites?
Comparing both pays off. Toyoko Inn's direct website consistently runs 5–8% lower than third-party OTAs, and APA Hotel's app sometimes offers flash deals. The same property can be $8–12 cheaper on one platform versus another, and OTA listings may add Tokyo's tiered accommodation tax (¥100–¥200 per person nightly) at checkout.
