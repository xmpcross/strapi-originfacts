## Q1: What is the cheapest way to fly to Southeast Asia from the US?
Book the long-haul into a major gateway — Singapore, Bangkok, or Hong Kong — where carrier competition is fiercest, then add intra-region tickets on AirAsia, Scoot, or VietJet. LAX–Singapore on Singapore Airlines typically runs $900–$1,200 round-trip in shoulder season, with Singapore–Siem Reap legs often under $120 one-way.

## Q2: When are the cheapest months to fly to Southeast Asia in 2026?
Late April through May, after Songkran ends April 15 and before the monsoon, and September to early November, between Indonesia's peak and the Christmas surge. Expect $700–$900 round-trip from the US West Coast to Bangkok. Avoid Chinese New Year week (February 17, 2026), Japan's Golden Week overlap, and the fortnight around Christmas.

## Q3: Does positioning through Tokyo or Seoul save money?
Often, yes. Sub-$600 LAX–Tokyo fares are common in ZIPAIR sales, and onward Seoul–Bangkok or Tokyo–Singapore legs run $250–$350 one-way, frequently totaling less than a single-ticket booking. One March example: LAX–Narita on ZIPAIR for $480 plus Narita–Singapore on Scoot for $310, versus $1,380 on United.

## Q4: How cheap are flights between Southeast Asian cities?
Very. A four-stop AirAsia loop priced in October came to roughly $370: Kuala Lumpur–Bali about $75, Bali–Jakarta about $55, Jakarta–Manila about $110, and Manila–Kuala Lumpur about $130. Vietnam works as a hub too, with Ho Chi Minh City–Bangkok at $50–$90 and Hanoi–Siem Reap often under $100.

## Q5: When do Southeast Asian airlines release flight sales?
Asian low-cost carriers refresh fares on local business hours, so timing matters more than the old US "book on Tuesday" rule. AirAsia drops weekly sales most Mondays at midnight Kuala Lumpur time, while VietJet's flash sales typically hit Tuesday and Thursday mornings in Hanoi. App-only fares run 10–15% below web prices.
