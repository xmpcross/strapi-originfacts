# FAQ proposal — Cheap Flights for Families: Smart Ways to Save on Group Travel

## Q1: When should families book flights for the best prices?
Domestic US trips are best booked one to three months out and international trips two to six months ahead. For school breaks, book spring break by mid-January, summer by February or March, Thanksgiving by late September, and Christmas by early October. Waiting until May can add $400–600 per seat on Europe fares.

## Q2: Are budget airlines actually cheaper for families?
Often not. A $79 ultra-low-cost fare can reach $180 per person after carry-on, seat assignment, and checked bag fees, and one cancelled flight without an interline agreement means buying new walk-up tickets. A useful rule: unless a budget airline is at least $60 per person cheaper than Southwest or JetBlue after fees, it is not actually cheaper.

## Q3: Do airlines have to seat children next to their parents for free?
Since April 2024, the US Department of Transportation's family seating commitment requires Alaska, American, Frontier, and JetBlue to seat children 13 and under next to an accompanying adult at no charge when seats are available at booking; United, Delta, Southwest, and Hawaiian have similar policies. The protection generally applies only when everyone shares one reservation.

## Q4: Can points and miles really cut family flight costs?
Even a modest points balance helps: one Chase Sapphire Preferred sign-up bonus of 60,000–75,000 points can cover four domestic round-trips via Southwest. The Southwest Companion Pass lets one designated person fly nearly free for the rest of that year plus the next. Finding four or five award seats together is the hardest part, so search when schedules open.

## Q5: Should a baby fly as a lap infant or in a purchased seat?
Children under two fly free in a lap on domestic US flights, while most airlines charge 10 percent of the adult fare plus taxes internationally. Lap travel works for flights under three hours, but buying a seat and bringing a car seat is recommended for transoceanic trips. Widebody bassinets exist but must be requested at booking.
