## Q1: Do you need an International Driving Permit to rent a car in Japan?
Yes — Japan accepts only the 1949 Geneva Convention IDP, not the 1968 Vienna version, and does not issue permits to visitors on arrival, so apply at home first. AAA issues the correct version in the US; the AA and Post Office issue it in the UK for around £6. Rental counters require both the IDP and the original license.

## Q2: How much does renting a car in Japan cost?
Economy Kei cars typically run under $40–$50 per day, standard compacts $50–$80, and seven-seater minivans often exceed $100. Add roughly $5 per day for an ETC card rental and $10–$20 for the strongly recommended NOC insurance upgrade. In Okinawa's shoulder season, intense competition often puts compact cars under $40 per day.

## Q3: Is an ETC card worth it for driving in Japan?
Yes. Japan's expressways are tolled, and costs add up — a three-day Hokkaido loop through Sapporo, Furano, Biei, and Asahikawa can run $30–$50 in tolls. An ETC card lets drivers pass toll gates without stopping and unlocks NEXCO weekend discount programs cutting expressway costs by up to 30 percent. Request it at booking, not at pickup.

## Q4: Which regions of Japan are best for a self-drive trip?
Hokkaido is the single most compelling destination, with straight, well-signed roads and scenery like Furano's July lavender fields — budget five to seven days. Okinawa's outer islands essentially require a car, Kyushu offers the Yamanami Highway between Aso and Yufuin, and Tohoku is underrated for autumn foliage. Skip driving in Tokyo, Kyoto, and Osaka, where parking costs $20–$40 daily.

## Q5: When should you book a rental car in Japan?
Book at least two to three weeks ahead in peak periods — Golden Week from late April through early May, and the mid-October through early November foliage season in Hokkaido and Tohoku, when inventory disappears fast. For Hokkaido in July or Okinawa in October, check availability about eight weeks out, when inventory tightens and prices start climbing.
