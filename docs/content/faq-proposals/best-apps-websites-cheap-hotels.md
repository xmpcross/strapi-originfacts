# FAQ proposal — Best Apps and Websites for Cheap Hotels in 2025 (Tested)

## Q1: What is the best website to start a cheap hotel search?
No single app is cheapest every time, so the recommended starting point is a meta-search engine rather than a booking site. Google Hotels is described as the best starting point in 2025, with a clean map view, deal badges, a working free-cancellation filter, and hotel-direct rates shown alongside OTAs like Booking and Expedia.

## Q2: Is booking directly with a hotel cheaper than using an OTA?
Often, yes, for major chains. Member-only rates are usually 5–10% below public rates, and Hilton, Marriott, and IHG honor price-match forms if an OTA is cheaper for the same room and terms. Direct bookings also earn points and elite-night credit. For independent hotels, an OTA is almost always cheaper or equal.

## Q3: When is the best time to book a hotel?
It depends on the market. Peak-season resort destinations should be booked three to six months out because last-minute prices rise or sell out. Urban business hotels are best booked one to four weeks ahead, since rates often drop as corporate demand finalizes. Leisure weekend city trips hit a sweet spot two to three weeks out.

## Q4: How can travelers save an extra 10–20% on hotel bookings?
Stacking discounts on top of the booking site delivers the extra savings. Layer a cashback portal such as Rakuten or TopCashback, card-linked offers from Amex, Chase, or Capital One, first-booking coupon codes of roughly 8–10%, and a travel credit card earning bonus points. A typical $180-per-night booking can land closer to $150.

## Q5: Is Airbnb cheaper than a hotel?
Not always. For solo travelers and couples staying one to three nights, hotels usually win once cleaning fees are added — a $90-per-night Airbnb with an $85 cleaning fee becomes a $175 one-night stay. For groups of four or more, stays of five-plus nights, or 28-plus-night bookings with 20–40% monthly discounts, rentals win decisively.
