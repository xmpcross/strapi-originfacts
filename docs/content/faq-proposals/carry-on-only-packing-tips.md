# FAQ proposal — Carry-On Only Packing: Travel Light Without Forgetting Essentials

## Q1: What are the carry-on size and weight limits for major airlines?
Carry-on limits vary widely by airline. United, American, Delta, and Alaska allow 22 x 14 x 9 inches, Southwest permits 24 x 16 x 10, and European carriers like British Airways and Lufthansa cap bags near 55 x 40 x 23 cm with an 8 kg weight limit that gates sometimes enforce.

## Q2: How much clothing should you pack for a carry-on-only trip?
Pack about one week of clothing regardless of trip length and plan laundry around day six or seven. A workable kit includes five t-shirts, two long-sleeve layers, a sweater or fleece, a rain shell, two pairs of pants, five pairs of underwear, and four pairs of socks, favoring merino pieces that can be re-worn two to three times.

## Q3: What is the liquids rule for carry-on luggage?
The TSA 3-1-1 rule caps liquids at 3.4 oz (100 ml) per container inside one quart-size clear bag, and the EU, UK, Japan, Australia, and Canada apply the same 100 ml limit. Although some airports with new CT scanners can theoretically allow more, enforcement is uneven, so packing to 100 ml remains the safe approach.

## Q4: Which bags work best for carry-on-only travel?
Proven options include the Peak Design Travel Backpack 45L ($299.95), which compresses to fit the 55 x 40 x 20 cm European cabin size, the Away Carry-On ($275) with a polycarbonate shell, and the Osprey Farpoint 40 or Fairview 40 (around $185). Avoid soft-sided bags over 45L when flying low-cost European carriers.

## Q5: How can you keep a carry-on bag under airline weight limits?
Wearing the heaviest and bulkiest items on the plane can cut three to four pounds from the bag — often the difference between passing a 7 kg weigh-in and arguing at the gate. That means flying in sneakers, jeans or hiking pants, a merino t-shirt with a sweater or fleece, and the rain shell if it is bulky.
