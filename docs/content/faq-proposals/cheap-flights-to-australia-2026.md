## Q1: When is the cheapest time to fly to Australia in 2026?
The cheapest windows are late April through mid-June, when West Coast fares to Sydney and Melbourne sit around $950–1,150, late August through the first week of October, and the first two weeks of November before Christmas pricing locks in. Avoid mid-December to late January, Easter week, and the July school holiday block.

## Q2: Which airlines offer the cheapest flights to Australia?
Carriers Americans often ignore consistently undercut Qantas and United. Fiji Airways via Nadi runs $850–1,050 round-trip from LAX, Philippine Airlines via Manila prices $200–400 below direct carriers, and China Airlines connects via Taipei on A350s. Scoot covers Singapore–Sydney for under $250 one-way for travelers already in Asia.

## Q3: Is it cheaper to fly into Brisbane than Sydney?
Yes — Sydney is the most expensive Australian airport to fly into by a meaningful margin. LAX–Brisbane runs $900–1,200 in shoulder season versus $1,050–1,400 for Sydney, and a Jetstar or Virgin Australia domestic hop onward costs $60–130 one-way and takes 90 minutes, potentially saving over $200 on the international ticket.

## Q4: How can points make business class to Australia affordable?
Cash business class from the West Coast typically costs $6,500–9,000 round-trip, so points transform the math. American AAdvantage books Qantas business at 80,000 miles one-way from LAX, Air Canada Aeroplan books Air New Zealand at around 87,500 points via Auckland, and Alaska Mileage Plan on Qantas allows one free stopover.

## Q5: Can you add a stopover on the way to Australia without paying more?
Several airlines turn connections into multi-day stopovers at little or no cost. Fiji Airways allows up to seven nights in Nadi at no fare premium, Air New Zealand makes Auckland stopovers routine and often free, and Singapore Airlines bundles a hotel night through its Singapore Stopover Holiday. Rules change, so confirm with the airline before booking.
