## Q1: When is the best time to book a flight?
Domestic flights are best booked one to three months ahead, with 54 days as the statistical sweet spot. International flights should be booked two to eight months in advance, and peak-season travel as early as possible, ideally three to six months out. Booking too early or too late both cost money.

## Q2: What day of the week is cheapest to fly?
Tuesdays and Wednesdays are typically 15–20% cheaper than weekend flights, and red-eye or early morning departures often cost significantly less. Flexibility matters most overall: shifting dates by even two to three days almost always uncovers better deals, and flexible dates can save 40–60% on the same route.

## Q3: Which flight search engines find the cheapest fares?
A combination works best: Google Flights for initial research and flexible dates, Skyscanner for budget airlines and multi-city routes, Momondo for quirky money-saving routings, and ITA Matrix for complex itineraries, though it doesn't allow direct booking. The airline's own website should always be checked before booking for exclusive deals or lower fees.

## Q4: Are budget airlines actually cheaper after fees?
Often, yes. Even with charges for carry-ons and seat selection, carriers like Spirit, Frontier, Ryanair, and AirAsia frequently come out ahead. Travelers with only a personal item who skip seat selection can pay 50–70% less than legacy carriers on the same route — but reading the fine print and totaling costs first is essential.

## Q5: Should you book when you see a good fare or wait?
Book when a fare sits 20–30% below average for the route and dates. Waiting for a perfect price often backfires — trying to save another $20 can mean prices jumping $100 overnight. Google Flights' price history shows whether a deal is genuinely good, and the cheapest seats go fast after alerts.
