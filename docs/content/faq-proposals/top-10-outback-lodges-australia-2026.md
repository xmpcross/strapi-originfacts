## Q1: How much does Longitude 131° cost per night?
Longitude 131° runs around AUD $3,200–$3,800 per person per night (roughly USD $2,100–$2,500) with a two-night minimum. The all-inclusive rate covers open-bar premium drinks, all meals including the Table 131° dune dinner, and small-group guided experiences at Uluru and Kata Tjuta, usually four to six guests per guide.

## Q2: When is the best time to visit Outback lodges in Australia?
Peak season runs May through September across most of the Red Centre and Top End, and it sells out early — May-to-September stays should be booked by January. Late April and early October shoulder weeks offer similar weather at 10–20% lower rates, while many tropical properties close entirely from roughly November to March.

## Q3: Are there budget-friendly Outback lodge options?
Kings Canyon Resort is the budget-conscious pick, with rooms typically AUD $300–$500 per night and glamping tents around $600, positioned at the trailhead of the 6km Rim Walk. Karijini Eco Retreat in Western Australia offers deluxe eco tents at roughly AUD $450–$550 per night, room only, inside the national park.

## Q4: Are Australian Outback lodges suitable for children?
Very few of these properties are family-friendly for children under ten. Bamurru Plains sets a minimum age of eight, and Longitude 131° has historically been adults-only on its signature dune dinner. Travellers with younger children should confirm age policies directly with each lodge before committing to a booking.

## Q5: How can you save money booking Australian Outback lodges?
Booking through Qantas Hotels or Virgin Australia channels earns points on several lodges, including Longitude 131° and El Questro, usually at the same cash price. Shoulder weeks in late April and early October cut 10–20% off peak rates, and operators like Aviair and Chartair can split one charter flight between two lodges.
